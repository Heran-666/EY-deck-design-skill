# Proposal Storyline policy

## 1. Audience outcome

Use this policy when the audience is being asked to select, approve, sponsor, fund, or authorize a proposed response, scope, approach, or commercial offer.

Define the decision sought, verified client purpose or tension, decision criteria or requirements, scope boundary, and evidence needed for credibility before drafting the Storyline. Never invent requirements, commitments, client facts, credentials, fees, schedules, capacity, or outcomes.

## 2. Storyline logic

Build the decision journey through:

`client purpose or tension → material challenges or principles → differentiated thesis → methodology → approach and work → deliverables → client value → credibility and fit → decision or next step`

Anchor the sequence in the decision, requirements, thesis, scope, approach, deliverables, value, and evidence. Make differentiation visible early and reinforce it through client value, methodology, approach, deliverables, and working style. Do not rely on a generic late-stage “why us” page unless the source requirements or approved Storyline warrant one.

Use protected placeholders for unverified client or EY evidence. Keep assumptions and unresolved scope questions review-only until confirmed.

## 3. Proposal context

Classify the proposal context internally before building the Storyline:

- `Formal RFP response`: the client issued explicit requirements, response instructions, evaluation criteria, or a mandatory sequence.
- `Client-development proposal`: no formal RFP governs the response; the proposal is shaped by the verified client need, desired decision, and the user's direction.
- `Hybrid`: an RFP or brief provides hard requirements while the user's strategic direction supplies a differentiated point of view.

Do not add this subtype to `content.md` unless the user explicitly requests client-facing wording that depends on it. Record it in `Core need`, `Scope boundaries`, or `Confirmed decisions` only when it materially governs the work.

### Formal RFP response

Treat mandatory requirements, response instructions, submission boundaries, and contractual constraints as hard constraints. Separate explicit scope and deliverables, desired outcomes, evaluation criteria, required evidence, mandatory formats or order, protected content, and unresolved contradictions.

Follow a mandatory order when one exists. Otherwise organize the response around the client's decision journey while maintaining complete coverage. Create a proposal-to-RFP mapping page only when requested or when it materially strengthens the client-facing story.

### Client-development proposal

Anchor the response in verified client context and the user's direction. Clarify the decision or sponsorship sought, business outcome or client problem, audience concerns, scope boundary, differentiating point of view, and credibility evidence. Do not infer requirements or turn an early hypothesis into a client fact.

### Hybrid

Use the RFP as the compliance floor and the user's direction as the strategic differentiator:

1. Identify the RFP's purpose and non-negotiable requirements.
2. Translate the user's direction into a proposal thesis, emphasis, and differentiated value.
3. Make the differentiation answer the RFP rather than compete with it.
4. Surface tensions between compliance and positioning for user resolution.

Treat the user's direction as a chosen angle, not objective client evidence; support it with facts, reasoning, methodology, or clearly labeled hypotheses.

## 4. Safeguards

- Distinguish RFP facts, user direction, researched evidence, inference, assumption, and recommendation.
- Preserve obligation-changing source wording and flag legal, regulatory, security, independence, commercial, contractual, or contradictory points.
- Do not claim that proposed work alone will solve issues outside the verified scope or control model.

## 5. Completion checks

Confirm that the Storyline makes the requested decision clear, covers mandatory requirements, connects the thesis to the approach and deliverables, expresses client value without unsupported claims, and identifies the evidence or protected placeholders required for credibility.
