# Other deliverable Storyline policy

## 1. Audience outcome

Use this policy when none of the named types fits. Replace `Other` with a specific form such as decision briefing, workshop, readout, update, report-out, or keynote, and define its success condition before drafting.

## 2. Storyline logic

Select the clearest logic justified by the audience outcome and source material:

- decision logic for a choice or approval;
- chronology for change over time;
- problem–solution for a bounded response to a verified problem;
- evidence–implication for a readout or findings review;
- comparison for alternatives or benchmarks;
- process for sequence, ownership, or operating flow;
- workshop flow for framing, participation, synthesis, and decisions.

State the selected logic in the Storyline thesis or narrative arc. Do not default to proposal logic merely because the deliverable seeks attention or action.

## 3. Form-specific contract

Before drafting, define:

- the named form and primary audience;
- the audience's starting point and desired change;
- the required inputs, constraints, and protected material;
- the expected ending, decision, artifact, or next step;
- any secondary behavior borrowed from another type policy.

Read one additional type policy only when a confirmed secondary behavior materially changes the Storyline.

## 4. Safeguards

- Do not leave the form as generic `Other`; use `Other: <specific form>`.
- Do not combine incompatible audience outcomes without surfacing the conflict.
- Do not borrow Proposal, Training, Sharing deck, or Interpretation mechanics as decoration.
- Do not claim outcomes beyond the verified scope or evidence.

## 5. Completion checks

Confirm that the named form, success condition, selected narrative logic, page sequence, and ending agree, and that every borrowed secondary behavior is necessary and explicit.
