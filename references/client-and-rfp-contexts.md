## 1. Select the context

Classify each request before building the storyline:

- `Formal RFP response`: the client has issued explicit requirements, response instructions, evaluation criteria, or a mandatory sequence.
- `Client-development proposal`: no formal RFP governs the response; the proposal is shaped primarily by the client need, desired decision, and the user's direction.
- `Hybrid`: an RFP or brief provides hard requirements while the user's strategic direction supplies a differentiated point of view that should reshape the narrative.

Use the classification internally. Do not add it to `content.md` unless the user explicitly requests client-facing wording that depends on it.

## 2. Formal RFP response

Treat mandatory requirements, response instructions, submission boundaries, and contractual constraints as hard constraints. Separate:

- Explicit deliverables and scope
- Core purpose and desired outcomes
- Evaluation criteria or likely decision logic
- Required evidence, credentials, team information, or commercial material
- Mandatory section order, formats, and protected content
- Ambiguities, contradictions, and missing inputs

Comply with a mandatory order when one exists. Otherwise reorganize the response around the client's decision journey while maintaining complete coverage. Do not create a separate coverage matrix unless the user requests one. A client-facing proposal-to-RFP mapping slide may be used when it strengthens the story or clarifies several core purposes.

## 3. Client-development proposal

When no formal RFP exists, infer neither requirements nor commitments. Anchor the proposal in verified client context and the user's direction. Clarify:

- The decision or sponsorship sought
- The business outcome or client problem
- The target audience and their likely concerns
- The proposed scope boundary
- The differentiating point of view
- The evidence required to make the proposal credible

Mark assumptions and unresolved scope questions as review-only. Do not turn an early hypothesis into a client fact.

## 4. Hybrid proposal

Use the RFP as the compliance floor and the user's direction as the strategic differentiator. Connect them explicitly:

1. Identify the RFP's core purpose and non-negotiable requirements.
2. Translate the user's direction into a proposal thesis, emphasis, and differentiated value.
3. Organize the storyline so the differentiation helps answer the RFP rather than competing with it.
4. Flag any tension between compliance and positioning for user resolution.

Do not treat the user's direction as objective client evidence. It is the proposal team's chosen angle and should be supported by facts, reasoning, methodology, or clearly labeled hypotheses.

## 5. Context safeguards

For every context:

- Distinguish RFP facts, user direction, researched evidence, inference, assumption, and recommendation.
- Use approved internal material for client/EY claims; otherwise insert precise placeholders.
- Preserve obligation-changing RFP wording and flag legal, regulatory, security, independence, commercial, contractual, or contradictory points for resolution.
