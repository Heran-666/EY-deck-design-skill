# Interpretation Storyline policy

## 1. Audience outcome

Use this policy when the primary goal is to explain and make sense of supplied material, findings, policy, research, data, or an existing document, with emphasis on meaning, implications, uncertainty, and response.

Define the source scope, intended audience, decisions or actions the interpretation may inform, and the boundary between source facts and the presentation team's analysis.

## 2. Storyline logic

Build the reasoning journey through:

`source and context → key findings or provisions → interpretation → audience implications → uncertainties and boundaries → response options or questions`

Group material by meaning and implication when source order is not mandatory. Preserve source hierarchy and obligation-changing language where it matters. Make the chain from evidence to interpretation to implication visible.

## 3. Safeguards

- Separate source fact, interpretation, inference, recommendation, and unresolved question.
- Preserve material qualifiers, exceptions, effective dates, scope, and uncertainty.
- Do not convert interpretation into legal, regulatory, financial, technical, or operational certainty without adequate support.
- Do not default to a proposal thesis or imply that the recommended response is already approved.

## 4. Completion checks

Confirm that material source points are represented accurately, every interpretation is traceable to its basis, implications are audience-specific, uncertainty is visible, and response options do not overstate the evidence.
